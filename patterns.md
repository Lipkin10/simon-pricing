# Patterns — techniques and price structures

## Profit-driver simulation
**When to use**: before any "grow share" or "cut cost" initiative.
**How**: take the P&L base; move price, variable cost, volume and fixed cost each by the same % in isolation; rank by profit delta.
**Trade-offs**: price moves are fastest and cheapest but the first thing competitors match. (ch05)

## Break-even volume check
**When to use**: every proposed cut, discount, promotion, free shipping, tax holiday.
**How**: contribution at new price; volume to hold profit = cut ÷ (margin − cut); compare with plausible elasticity (median ≈ 2).
**Trade-offs**: kills most promotions on paper — that is the point. (ch05, ch09)

## Fifty-fifty midpoint pricing
**When to use**: linear-ish demand; any cost, tax, FX or value shock.
**How**: estimate p_max (volume → 0) by expert judgment + conjoint; price = ½ (p_max + variable cost); pass on half of any change.
**Trade-offs**: needs a demand estimate; misses by ±15 % cost ~43 % of profit. (ch06)

## Demand-curve triangulation
**When to use**: any price decision larger than an increment.
**How**: combine at least two of: expert judgment ("one up, one down", written answers, then consensus), direct customer questions (never alone), conjoint (best survey method), field price tests, historical data (rarely valid).
**Trade-offs**: conjoint costs money and time; historical data is free and usually wrong. (ch06)

## Two-price differentiation with a fence
**When to use**: heterogeneous willingness to pay, yes-no purchases.
**How**: sort individual maximum prices; high price for the top block, low price for the next; fence by version, channel, access, timing, language or degraded low tier.
**Trade-offs**: fence failure drops profit below the uniform price; each extra tier adds less. (ch07)

## Nonlinear pricing (first unit dearest)
**When to use**: same buyer takes several units or visits; usage can be tracked per customer.
**How**: for each unit index, find the profit-max price across segments; the descending schedule is the tariff.
**Trade-offs**: needs a named card/account; raises both volume and average price when done right (cinema +37.7 %). (ch07)

## Mixed bundling
**When to use**: item-level WTPs are heterogeneous.
**How**: bundle WTP = sum of item WTPs; set bundle below the sum; keep stand-alone items and re-optimize their prices.
**Trade-offs**: pure bundling grows risky as components standardize; à la carte forfeits WTP transfer. (ch07, ch08)

## Incremental volume discount
**When to use**: any B2B rate table.
**How**: apply each discount step only to units above the step, not to the whole order.
**Trade-offs**: buyers will ask for full-volume; same table doubles seller profit. (ch07)

## Two-part tariff (BahnCard type)
**When to use**: customer compares only variable out-of-pocket cost with a rival; headline cut impossible.
**How**: fixed fee + discounted usage price set below the rival's perceived variable cost; tiers by usage; model heavy-user cannibalization and antitrust exposure.
**Trade-offs**: perceived discount > real discount is the gain; killing it later causes revolt. (ch02, ch08)

## Pay per use / outcome metric
**When to use**: value can be metered cheaply; product improvement cannot be pushed through the old price anchor.
**How**: pick the unit of customer benefit, install at low upfront cost, price the unit so better performance converts into revenue automatically; bundle operation into it.
**Trade-offs**: seller carries asset and performance risk; needs measurement infrastructure. (ch08)

## New price metric
**When to use**: your advantage is hidden by the current unit (weight, hours) and visible in another (area, outcome).
**How**: enumerate feasible metrics; compute the competitive gap under each; switch to the one where you win.
**Trade-offs**: acceptance depends on novelty and market position. (ch08)

## New price parameter (Sanifair)
**When to use**: a costly free service consumed by non-buyers.
**How**: upgrade the service, charge at point of use, exempt protected groups, refund most as a voucher redeemable only with you.
**Trade-offs**: zero-to-nonzero fees need research first or they churn customers. (ch08)

## Freemium
**When to use**: marginal cost ≈ 0, experience good.
**How**: attractive free tier for reach; fence by feature/version/usage so first-timers convert; loyalty design for repeat; manage on conversion rate and premium lifetime value.
**Trade-offs**: free too rich kills conversion, too poor kills reach; price moves are ~revenue-neutral. (ch08)

## Profit-based sales incentive
**When to use**: discounts drift, price increases fail.
**How**: commission rises as granted discount falls; show the live commission on the rep's screen; keep margins confidential.
**Trade-offs**: form matters less than the fact that profit, not revenue, drives pay. (ch05, ch08)

## Intelligent surcharge
**When to use**: cost pass-through, peak damping, extra value (space, speed, availability).
**How**: keep the elastic headline price; add low-attention components: unbundled item, new component, indexed surcharge, time/place/person differentiation.
**Trade-offs**: never on something felt as already paid. (ch08)

## Below-radar price increase
**When to use**: complex price systems (fees, parts, assortments).
**How**: inventory every price element with transactions; survey the front line for perceived sensitivity; raise the invisible, low-elasticity items; split exclusive from commodity parts.
**Trade-offs**: slow, granular; zero complaints when done right. (ch09)

## Discount in goods
**When to use**: crisis concession, B2B, durables, leases.
**How**: keep nominal price; add units, services or free months; label as a temporary crisis measure.
**Trade-offs**: needs spare capacity; reversibility is the reward. (ch09)

## Less Expensive Alternative (LEA)
**When to use**: low-price imitators attack a premium brand.
**How**: second brand ~20 % below, minimal service, bulk-only, longer lead times; measure complementarity vs cannibalization.
**Trade-offs**: dilutes focus; far cheaper than cutting the lead brand. (ch04)

## Three-option menu with anchor
**When to use**: buyers lack a reference; a two-tier menu skews cheap.
**How**: add a high anchor that need not sell and a mid-tier or same-price decoy; design the range so the intended tier is the middle.
**Trade-offs**: extreme anchors repel; lab effects to be tested, not assumed. (ch03)

## Price leadership and legal signaling
**When to use**: symmetric oligopoly, overcapacity, price war risk.
**How**: announce intentions publicly to all participants (never conditional on a rival); announce purpose and duration of any large temporary cut; keep sales force consistent with the message.
**Trade-offs**: legal review on every signal; one self-destructive competitor is enough to sink it. (ch06, ch09)

## Price → market-cap translation
**When to use**: selling a pricing program to owners, board or a PE buyer.
**How**: Δprice → Δafter-tax profit × PE; lock price gains into contracts before valuation; price per unit, not across the board.
**Trade-offs**: assumes the increase sticks and PE holds. (ch10)
