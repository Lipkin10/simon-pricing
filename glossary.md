# Glossary

**À la carte pricing** — selling bundle components individually; forfeits the bundle's WTP transfer (Ch 8)
**Anchor effect** — buyers without a reference adopt any number placed before them, experts included (Ch 3)
**Anti-discount incentive** — commission that rises as the granted discount falls (Ch 5, Ch 8)
**Asymmetry of price decisions** — bad price moves hit share price at once, good ones pay slowly (Ch 10)
**Borrowing from the future** — a promotion pulling forward sales that would have happened at full price (Ch 5)
**Breakage** — prepaid balances never redeemed, booked as profit (Ch 8)
**Break-even volume** — fixed cost ÷ (price − variable unit cost); the volume a cut must reach to hold profit (Ch 5)
**Bundling, pure / mixed** — several products at one price below the sum; mixed keeps stand-alone items too (Ch 7)
**Buyer's market** — crisis state: low utilization, rising inventory, power with customers (Ch 9)
**Chamberlin vs Cournot** — oligopoly outcomes: mutual following (high price) vs mutual ignoring (falling price) (Ch 6)
**Chief Pricing Officer** — divisional pricing role (GE 2001) that raised target-price attainment (Ch 10)
**Consumer surplus** — gap between a buyer's maximum price and the price paid (Ch 7)
**Contribution margin** — price minus variable unit cost (Ch 5)
**Cost-plus pricing** — cost × markup; ignores willingness to pay and volume response (Ch 5, Ch 6)
**Cross-price elasticity** — % own volume change ÷ % competitor price change (Ch 6)
**Discount in goods** — concession as extra units/services; valued at retail by customer, costs variable cost (Ch 9)
**EDLP vs Hi-Lo** — constant low prices vs alternating promotions; never mix (Ch 4, Ch 7)
**Effective discount** — real average saving after fixed fees vs the perceived headline discount (Ch 2)
**Endowment effect** — owners value a good far above what non-owners will pay (Ch 3)
**Fencing** — separation that keeps high-WTP buyers from the low price: version, channel, access, time, degraded tier (Ch 7, Ch 8)
**Fifty-fifty rule** — optimal price = ½ (maximum price + variable cost); pass on half of any change (Ch 6)
**Flat rate** — fixed price per period, unlimited use; light users subsidize heavy users (Ch 8)
**Floor price** — internal minimum; in practice the price nearly every deal closes at (Ch 10)
**Freemium** — free basic tier plus paid premium; a penetration strategy for experience goods (Ch 8)
**Full-volume vs incremental discount** — discount on the whole order vs only units above each step (Ch 7)
**Gray market / parallel imports** — resale from low-price to high-price segment or country (Ch 4, Ch 7)
**Hidden Champions** — little-known global leaders priced 10–15 % above market (Ch 10)
**Hog cycle** — delayed supply response causing commodity price oscillation (Ch 2)
**Information / profit cliff** — differentiation from thin WTP data that overshoots and collapses profit (Ch 7)
**Jawboning** — public advocacy of value competition over price competition (Ch 9)
**Key pricing indicator** — own price relative to average competitor price at each aggregation level (Ch 10)
**Less Expensive Alternative (LEA)** — stripped second brand ~20 % below the lead brand (Ch 4)
**Magic of the middle** — uninformed buyers pick the middle of a price range (Ch 3)
**Margin deception** — a high unit margin hiding an unattainable volume requirement (Ch 5)
**Market design** — auction rules engineered for goals beyond maximum price (Ch 8)
**Massification** — exclusive brand degraded by volume growth (Ch 4)
**Mental accounting** — spending booked to separate mental accounts with different price sensitivity (Ch 3)
**Moon price** — list price nobody pays; anchor that dies when overused (Ch 3)
**Name your own price / reverse pricing** — buyer bids, seller accepts above a secret threshold; for excess capacity only (Ch 8)
**Nonlinear pricing** — price per unit varies with quantity; first unit dearest (Ch 6, Ch 7)
**Overcapacity** — root cause of persistent price pressure and price wars (Ch 9)
**Pantry effect** — customers stock up on deals and wait for the next one (Ch 5)
**Pay per use** — charging for delivered performance, not the asset (Ch 8)
**Pay what you want** — buyer sets price, seller must accept; avoid (Ch 8)
**Penetration vs skimming** — low launch price to seed adoption vs high launch price stepping down (Ch 7)
**Penny gap** — the hardest freemium step: from zero to any positive price (Ch 8)
**Placebo effect of price** — higher price improves measured performance or experience (Ch 3)
**Praktiker syndrome** — identity built on a permanent discount claim with no exit (Ch 10)
**Prestige (Veblen) effect** — demand rising with price within a range; ~5 % of cases (Ch 3)
**Price as quality indicator** — buyers infer quality from price when they cannot judge it (Ch 3)
**Price elasticity** — % volume change ÷ % price change, sign dropped; typical 1.3–3 (Ch 5, Ch 6)
**Price leadership** — one player moves first and the industry follows (Ch 6, Ch 10)
**Price metric** — the unit the price is expressed in, distinct from how it is collected (Ch 3, Ch 8)
**Price positioning** — low / ultra-low / premium / luxury; set at founding, rarely reversible (Ch 4)
**Price-response function** — the demand curve mapping price to units sold (Ch 6)
**Price taker** — seller who must accept the market's price; a structurally weak model (Ch 1)
**Price threshold** — kink in demand at round numbers; go to .99 or test crossing (Ch 3)
**Price transparency** — internet and scanning apps collapsing spatial fences (Ch 8)
**Price war** — value transfer from all players to customers; started by overcapacity and share targets (Ch 9, Ch 10)
**Pricing power** — ability to achieve desired prices; +35 % with top-management framework (Ch 2, Ch 10)
**Prisoner's dilemma** — a cut matched by rivals gains nothing and destroys margin (Ch 6, Ch 9)
**Profit rectangle / triangle** — uniform-price profit vs the total attainable with perfect differentiation (~2×) (Ch 7)
**Prospect theory** — losses hurt more than equal gains please; path to the net result matters (Ch 3)
**Relative value of growth (RVG)** — value of 1 % revenue growth vs 1 % margin; must split price from volume growth (Ch 10)
**Reservation / maximum price** — price equal to perceived value; WTP in a yes-no purchase (Ch 6)
**Revenue management / yield management** — allocating perishable capacity to price points from forecasts (Ch 7)
**Second-order benefit** — downstream outcome in money (energy savings, fewer accidents) that moves B2B buyers (Ch 2)
**Signaling** — lawful public announcement of intentions, never conditional on a competitor (Ch 6, Ch 9)
**Source-of-demand test** — after a spike: new buyers, taken from rivals, or pulled forward? (Ch 5)
**Surcharge, intelligent** — low-attention price component: unbundled item, new component, indexed pass-through, differentiation (Ch 8)
**Taxi-meter effect** — disutility of watching charges accrue; flat rates remove it (Ch 8)
**Three profit drivers** — price, volume, cost (variable and fixed) (Ch 5)
**Two-part / two-dimensional tariff** — fixed fee plus discounted usage price (Ch 2, Ch 8)
**Value to customer** — perceived, subjective value; the only driver of willingness to pay (Ch 2, Ch 4)
**Versioning** — constant price, rising performance each generation; skimming's complement (Ch 7)
**Vickrey auction** — second-price auction that induces bidders to reveal true WTP (Ch 7, Ch 8)
**Yes-no vs variable-quantity purchase** — one unit per buyer vs quantity depending on price (Ch 6, Ch 7)
